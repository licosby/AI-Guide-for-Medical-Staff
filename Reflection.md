
# **Most Important Decision**

The biggest decision I made during this project was finally caving in and letting Google Gemini help me put everything together. I tried so hard to do everything myself because I wanted to be able to say “I built this from scratch,” but the truth is that I would not have been able to complete the MVP I had planned without using a coding assistant. There is a lot to learn in any form of coding, and when you have never done the advanced parts you are trying to include, you eventually have to admit you need help. Once I accepted that, everything changed. Gemini took what I had been struggling with and turned it into the version of the project I originally imagined when I wrote my proposal. It did not replace me. It helped me get past the parts I did not have the experience for yet, and it let me focus on the design, the flow, and the logic instead of getting stuck on the technical pieces.

# **What Worked**

Starting with the static site worked really well for me. Walking through the thought process with Copilot helped me figure out what I actually wanted because I do not always know the right coding jargon. Being able to say “I want point A to move to point B” and then seeing it happen made it easier to go back and say “No, I meant the other part B.” That back and forth helped me understand my own project better. By the time I moved everything over to Gemini, I had already clarified most of the confusing parts. I have used Gemini for other projects, and I am learning that the more words, directions, and expectations I give it — and in the simplest form possible — the better the results are. I get what I want with less debugging, and I can spend more time iterating on styles and features instead of trying to fix broken logic.

# **What I Would Do Differently**

This is actually hard for me to answer because I liked the way I approached this project. If I had to pick something, I probably would have spent more time refining my wireframes. Having the exact home screen layout ready made everything easier once I sent it to Gemini. I also probably would not have jumped straight into wanting to build something for the medical field. I did not know anything about APIs, and it took me a week just to figure out what I needed to make my logic work. Then it took even longer to figure out how to implement it. That was the point where I turned to Gemini and told it what logic I wanted and what APIs I needed access to, and it went to work.

# **What I Learned About Building for the Web**

I learned that building for the web is a lot more about problem solving than memorizing code. You have to be willing to try things, break things, and then fix them. You also have to be patient with yourself because even small mistakes can take hours to track down. I spent three hours trying to find one line of CSS during Project 01. That taught me that sometimes the best thing you can do is step back and rethink your approach instead of fighting with the same problem over and over.

# **What I Learned About Myself**

I learned that I will do whatever it takes to figure out how to complete whatever I decide to make. I really enjoy designing apps with coding agents. I like being able to write out what I want, how I want it to flow, and then see it come to life. If something does not work, I do not have to spend five days trying to fix it. I can just say “Let’s do this instead,” and it pivots. It makes the whole process feel more creative and less stressful.

# **What I Learned About the Tools**

I learned that vibe coding is not a bad thing, and it does not mean someone is lazy. I spent plenty of time and had plenty of conversations with Copilot and Gemini to get this app where it is now. I know design is all about iteration, but I honestly feel like this project is perfect for what it is supposed to be. I am not a medical professional, so from my point of view there is nothing else I could add to make it better. If this were my portfolio, I would keep changing things forever, but because this is meant to be clinical and streamlined, the style needs to stay clean. If I changed anything, it would probably be the icon or logo design because I did not spend much time thinking about it. I might also change the name to something more catchy if I were pitching it to a company.
